How to build impact at your Node level

  • Identify a working group and designate a focal point (e.g. an Impact Officer) to lead the effort.
  • Agree on the goals and timeline for your impact initiative.
    • Examples of priorities:
  • Begin developing draft impact pathways and gathering information on activities and outputs.
  • Review past efforts, such as funder requests, project proposals or existing metrics for indicators.
  • Contact the Hub and relevant Nodes to explore impact methodologies and resources.
  • Start reaching out to your Node members to build impact cases.
  • Define a preliminary methodology and begin collecting data for indicators.
    • Examples of methodologies:
  • Revisit and update your draft pathways when preparing proposals or responding to funder requests.
  • Share your impact stories and indicators internally within your Node and externally across ELIXIR.

  • Assist colleagues in your Node with building their impact stories, and seek support from the Hub or other Nodes when needed.

How to build impact in your Platform level

  • Motivate the group by finding the right time and purpose—such as for a white paper, planning a new program or wrapping up an old one.
  • Define impact pathways in face-to-face meetings or a series of platform discussions. Group activities accordingly and seek support from the ELIXIR Hub.
  • Identify indicators: Review existing ones or establish new, crucial metrics.
  • Assign responsibility for tracking different indicators.
  • Develop an action plan for creating and refining these indicators.
  • Showcase your impact through slides and white papers, and seek feedback from impact experts, including the Hub and the Impact Group.
  • Revisit and refine your indicators and impact pathways as needed.

Demonstrate your impact to funders and ESFRI requests

ELIXIR Nodes and the Hub have a long history of providing impact information to funders, ministries, policymakers and ESFRI-related processes. However, the information and formulations developed for previous requests have often been dispersed across applications, reports, evaluations and other documents, making them difficult to find and reuse.

The Node Support Commissioned Service WP3 (2024-2026)—Improving preparedness for funding applications and other requests for information from funders and stakeholders—is addressing this gap by developing an internal knowledge base of impact information for funders.

The objective is to improve access across ELIXIR to existing impact evidence, formulations, references and supporting resources. The knowledge base is intended to help Nodes prepare written materials for funding applications, reviews and stakeholder communication without having to begin each response from scratch.

The resource includes:

  • examples of previous formulations taken from publicly available documents;
  • references, citations and descriptions of resources that can support impact claims;
  • ELIXIR-wide and Node-specific impact evidence;
  • frequently asked questions from funders and policymakers;
  • links to relevant reports, evaluations, publications and other supporting sources;
  • guidance for producing clear, consistent and audience-specific responses;
  • showcase examples illustrating how Nodes have adapted impact evidence to their national contexts.

The content is organised around recurring areas of ELIXIR impact, such as:

  • research efficiency;
  • bioinformatics resource uptake;
  • scientific legacy;
  • human capital and skills development;
  • relationship and collaboration capital;
  • equal opportunities;
  • policy influence;
  • public awareness;
  • sustainability of research infrastructures.

All statements included in the knowledge base should be linked to an identifiable source. References and citations are therefore an integral part of the methodology, allowing users to verify the evidence, understand its original context and avoid unsupported or misleading claims.

The texts should be treated as suggestions and sources of inspiration, not as fixed templates. Users remain responsible for selecting, updating and adapting the material to the specific question, audience, funding programme and national or institutional context.

The methodological principles are:

  • start from the exact information requested by the funder or stakeholder;
  • identify the relevant impact area and supporting evidence;
  • distinguish between ELIXIR-wide evidence and Node-, Platform-, Community- or service-specific evidence;
  • verify that figures, examples, policy references and membership information are current;
  • retain links and citations to the original source;
  • adapt the wording to the purpose and audience rather than copying it without context;
  • combine shared ELIXIR narratives with national or local examples;
  • avoid overstating attribution or using evidence beyond what the source can support.

The underlying compilation is designed as a living database that can be updated from a single point and used to feed different user-facing resources. These may include searchable and filterable tables, as well as automatically generated and formatted reports. This approach makes the information easier to access and maintain while allowing the resource to grow as Nodes contribute new impact evidence, references and examples.

The aim is not to produce identical responses across ELIXIR, but to provide a reliable and referenced starting point from which Nodes and the Hub can build clear, credible and context-appropriate impact narratives.

Working with economists

External assessments can provide novel perspectives and insights that may not be readily available to internal assessors. However, external assessments are often costly and time-consuming and may not always account for the unique contextual nuances and priorities of the institution being evaluated. The Cost-Benefit Analysis approach compares the costs and benefits of a policy or project to determine whether the benefits outweigh the costs. The analysis considers both quantitative and qualitative factors to make an informed decision. All benefits and costs are expressed in monetary terms, even if the effects are not only financial. The approach is often used by governments and economists to evaluate the impact of various investment projects. It is reliable for comparison of positive and negative impacts and has the potential to capture many effects of RIs. The approach, however, can be costly and time-consuming. There is also a limited explanation of causality and it can be challenging to capture all costs. (https://www.esfri.eu/sites/default/files/ESFRI_Impact_Policy_Brief_23052023.pdf)
