How to wear an impact hat

Understand what it means to work on impact, when and how to approach it. This is an introduction to the basic concept of the impact mandate for a Research Infrastructure, helping you define what impact means in your context and how to start building your approach.
Status
in development
Primary audience
Head of Nodes, Nodes Coordinators, Impact officers, Communication officers, Technical coordinators, Platform leaders, Product managers, Service managers
  1. Start thinking about impact and what this means for your work
  2. Identify the collaborators for this journey
  3. Understand the importance and limitations of engaging with external stakeholders and colleagues on impact

When to wear this hat

Wear the impact hat continuously if you are an impact officer or occasionally if it is a part-time responsibility. While roles like Heads of Nodes, Node Coordinators, or Communication Officers often share impact duties, impact is everyone’s responsibility. A collective effort ensures accuracy and avoids assumptions that could lead to incomplete or incorrect demonstrations. Ideally, we need technical experts and people with high level understanding of research priorities at the national and international level to work together.

As scientists and research infrastructure managers, you often receive national and international funding, which means you will be asked to demonstrate the impact of your work to funders—whether for past or future funding. However, funders, policymakers, and review panels may not fully understand research infrastructures, leading to requests that don’t always align with the ELIXIR context. In such cases, you must find alternative ways to justify your impact and, when necessary, educate stakeholders about the challenges of their requests. Keep impact in mind during meetings or conversations with colleagues, and start proactively gathering impact cases—most can remain as ad-hoc stories, while others may develop into larger narratives.

What does it mean to wear this hat

Wearing the impact hat means coordinating efforts to simplify complex information and communicate the big picture of your work’s impact on science, research, society, economy, and innovation. Research infrastructures are often taken for granted by users, similar to road infrastructure, which is a sign that things are operating smoothly, though that makes them to be often overlooked until they fail or disappear due to funding cuts. Since they are publicly funded, demonstrating impact is a mandate to sustain funding. That’s why demonstrating impact should be a collective effort that everyone should contribute and eventually benefit from.

Finally, as collecting impact related information can be complicated but is a “must” as discussed above, it is important to get as much as possible out of it. Maximize the value of collected impact data by:

  • Showcasing statements or metrics on your institute’s webpage.
  • Reusing impact-related text in grant applications.

How others see you

As someone wearing the impact hat, you may be seen as an expert—a go-to person for advice on impact demonstrations. However, your expertise depends on experience, collaborations, and depth of impact work. Building your experience is crucial and you can do this by:

  • Reading resources (e.g. ELIXIR has a lot of information online, see the boxes “useful reads” at the end of each chapter).
  • Connecting with internal (e.g. ELIXIR Impact Group; find link in the box at the end of the chapter) or external experts (e.g. economists).

Challenges to anticipate:

  • Colleagues may see your requests for impact information as a burden. Mitigate this by explaining the benefits or offering tangible returns (e.g. data showing how their work helps others).
  • External audiences (funders, policymakers, other researchers) often do not understand what a research infrastructure is, therefore it is important to use analogies (e.g. roads, telescopes) if needed to clarify differences and demonstrate challenges and your value, but be mindful of nuances lost in translation.
  • Considering how hard it is to demonstrate and quantify our societal impact, we are asked about our return on investment which then means that we should work with economists, which is an expensive solution.

What is your style

Impact is about connecting small details to the big picture. There is no single “right” way to do this, but only mechanisms and terminologies that can help you understand and demonstrate impact. Once you have developed your approach:

  • Don’t hesitate—test your logic, seek feedback from experts (e.g. Impact Group; find link in the box at the end of the chapter), and revisit and refine your approach and methodology.
  • Communicate openly with colleagues to encourage participation. The more people understand your focus, the easier it is to gather information.

Best practices:

  • Reference methodologies (e.g. ELIXIR Toolkit, Impact Pathway Logic; see next chapter) and explain your conclusions.
  • Align with existing impact frameworks in your institute to maintain consistency for external stakeholders.
  • Leverage past work (e.g. previous funder responses) to build on established understanding.
